In an emergency

If there is a fire

  1. Shout to wake everyone and leave by the nearest exit. Don't stop for belongings.
  2. Meet at the meeting point, then count everyone.
  3. Call 999 from outside. Read them the address on page 3.
  4. Don't go back in. Use the fire blanket (kitchen wall) only on a small pan fire, and only if it's safe; otherwise just get out.

Exits: front door; doors to the decking. Smoke alarms: where. The plan is on page 5.

If you smell gas

  1. Put out any flame and don't touch light switches or plugs.
  2. Turn the gas off at the bottle: where the bottles and their valves are.
  3. Open the doors and windows and go outside.
  4. Call the gas supplier's emergency line, number from the bottle label, then Trina.

If the carbon monoxide alarm sounds

It's where the CO alarm is. Open the doors and windows, get everyone outside and call Trina. Headache, dizziness or sickness: call 999.

The gas safety certificate is pinned where the gas safety record is displayed.

Hot tub

Opening and closing the lid

  1. Pull the rope by the wooden pillar: the first half of the lid lifts.
  2. From inside the tub, push the second half up.
  3. To close, pull the rope: it brings both halves down. Close it every time you get out.

Keep it safe

  • Use the toilet and shower first. No fake tan, suntan lotion or skin cream, and no soap or oil in the water.
  • Stay in 15 minutes at a time, then get out and cool down.
  • Not after a heavy meal, alcohol or sedatives.
  • Keep your head above the water, and long hair away from the drains.
  • One person per seat.
  • Children only with an adult, and no children under 4.
  • Pregnant, unwell, or on medicine for your heart: ask a doctor first.
  • No glass in or near the tub.

Put the lid back down when you get out. We set the temperature. Water cloudy or cold? Don't add anything and don't use it; text Trina and we'll change it.

The sea

Lifeguards
None from September until spring. If anyone is in trouble in the water, call 999 and ask for the Coastguard.
The beach
Shingle, with sand when the tide is far out. Check the day's tide times for Worthing before you swim or walk out on the sand.
Water quality
Goring, the nearest tested beach, was rated Good in 2025. In summer, the Environment Agency's Swimfo page gives the day's reading.

Step-free by the sea

Worthing seafront, 12 minutes by car, is the easiest. Free beach wheelchairs, for an adult and a child, are lent from Splashpoint on the seafront: book ahead on the council's website, and bring help, as the shingle is steep. There is a Changing Places toilet on the promenade opposite the Dome, 9am to 5pm.

With a Blue Badge, parking is free for up to 3 hours in the disabled bays of Worthing's open-air council car parks.
